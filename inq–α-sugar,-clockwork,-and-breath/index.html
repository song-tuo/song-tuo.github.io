    INQ–α: Sugar, Clockwork, and Breath

    A Computational Translation of Music Technical guidance: Tim Rodenbröker
    Personal Project
    Tools & Technologies: Processing, MIDI data parsing, JSON data structures, algorithmic animation, mathematical mapping functions, generative geometry

    Introduction
    This project originates from the music of my final ballet performance, set to the overture of The Nutcracker. After stepping away from ballet, I returned to this score not to reenact the performance, but to re-encode it computationally. By translating the music into data and algorithmic rules, the project examines how a bodily, time-based experience can be transformed into a generative system—one that preserves rhythm and structure without relying on figurative representation.

    Concept
    The project considers music as a system of discrete events unfolding over time, and performance as a synchronization between body and temporal structure. Here, the choreography is absent, but its logic remains embedded in the score.

        Three metaphors organize the system:
    • Sugar — accumulation and crystallization, corresponding to moments of musical density
    • Clockwork — cyclical precision and repetition, reflecting rhythmic structure
    • Breath — expansion, contraction, and pause, recalling the physical act of dancing

    Rather than illustrating emotion or narrative, the project encodes these qualities through computational relationships. Musical attributes become parameters; time becomes a controlling variable; form emerges as a consequence of data-driven rules.

    (The musical score comes from the Russian National Museum of Music; the movements of the suite were handwritten by a copyist)
    Data Origin & Translation
    The source data comes directly from the music used in my final ballet performance. The translation process unfolds in several stages:

    • Musical Score → MIDI
             The ballet music is converted into a MIDI file. Beyond pitch, MIDI captures timing, velocity, and duration—elements essential to both musical phrasing and       
              bodily movement.
    • MIDI → JSON
             To make the data accessible in Processing, the MIDI file is parsed and converted into JSON format. Each note becomes an object containing its pitch, 
              velocity, duration, and onset time.
    • Pitch Normalization
            Original pitch values ranging from 0 to 47 are compressed into a 0–11 range by grouping identical pitch classes across different octaves. This preserves 
            harmonic relationships while simplifying the dataset for generative operations.

    The resulting dataset functions as a structured temporal array, where each value corresponds to a specific musical event.
